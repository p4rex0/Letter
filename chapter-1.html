Chapter 1: Glimpses of You

The first time I met her was on the first of May.

It was a day I had been looking forward to for weeks. We had decided to go clubbing together, and although we had talked before, I had never actually seen her in person. All I knew of her were the pictures she had shared. Somehow, those pictures had already made me curious, but curiosity was nothing compared to what I felt that evening.

We were supposed to meet around eight o'clock.

As the time got closer, I found myself checking my phone every few minutes. I tried to distract myself, but excitement has a way of making every minute feel longer than it actually is.

When I texted her to ask if she was on her way, she told me she would be there soon.

Then my phone rang.

"I'm here," she said.

My heart immediately began racing.

I started walking toward the meeting spot, trying to act calm even though I was anything but calm. From a distance, I saw a girl standing alone under the city lights.

She was wearing a black dress.

Her glasses rested gently on her face as she looked between her phone and the people passing by. Every now and then she glanced around, searching for someone.

Searching for me.

For a moment, I simply stood there.

The world around me seemed to fade into the background as I watched her.

Then I walked closer.

And that was when I truly saw her.

The pictures had never done her justice.

There was something effortlessly beautiful about her. Her eyes carried a warmth I couldn't explain. Her smile seemed capable of brightening the entire evening. Every feature of her face fit together perfectly, as though they had been carefully painted by an artist who understood beauty better than anyone else.

The black dress suited her perfectly.

She looked elegant, graceful, and somehow unreal.

Like a fairy that had accidentally wandered into the middle of an ordinary city.

I introduced myself, trying my best to hide how nervous I was.

She introduced herself with a smile.

That smile made things easier.

Not completely.

But enough.

We booked a cab and began our journey to the club.

At first, the conversation was light. She told me she wasn't familiar with the area and kept asking questions about whether it was safe. There was something oddly adorable about the way she asked them, as if she was trying to prepare herself for every possible situation.

Then she asked me a question that caught me completely off guard.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Sure."

"Are you Hindu?"

I laughed.

"Yes."

The relief on her face was immediate.

Apparently, because of the way I looked, she had convinced herself that I belonged to a completely different religion.

The two of us spent the next few minutes laughing about it.

It was one of those silly conversations that shouldn't have mattered.

Yet somehow it became one of the first moments that made me feel comfortable around her.

By the time we reached the club, the nervousness I had felt earlier had started to disappear.

While waiting for our entry, we talked about our families, our interests, and the things we enjoyed. That was when I learned how much she loved rave parties and techno music.

What surprised me most wasn't what she liked.

It was how easy it felt talking to her.

There are some people who make conversations feel like work.

With her, it felt effortless.

As though we were continuing a conversation that had begun years ago.

She was kind.

She was sincere.

And every minute spent talking to her made me want to know more.

When we finally entered the club, music filled every corner of the room.

The event was a mix of techno beats and Bollywood songs, creating an energy that seemed impossible to resist.

She started dancing immediately.

Confidently.

Happily.

Without caring who was watching.

I tried copying her dance moves and failed more often than I succeeded.

But she laughed.

And somehow that made it worth it.

The happiest version of her appeared when she danced.

Her smile became brighter.

Her eyes seemed to sparkle beneath the flashing lights.

Every time she laughed, I found myself smiling too.

I don't remember every song that played that night.

But I remember her.

I remember the way she moved with complete freedom.

I remember how happy she looked.

And I remember being unable to look away.

Hours passed before either of us noticed.

Eventually, the final song ended, and the night slowly began winding down.

We booked another cab and started heading home.

During the ride, she pulled out her earphones.

Without saying much, she handed one to me and kept the other for herself.

It was such a small gesture.

Yet it felt strangely intimate.

For the next part of the journey, we sat there listening to the same songs.

Sharing the same music.

Sharing the same silence.

And somehow that silence felt comfortable.

When we reached home, I gave her some clothes to change into.

A little later, she walked out wearing one of my oversized T-shirts.

I don't think she'll ever fully understand what that moment did to me.

It was something simple.

Something ordinary.

Yet my heart reacted as though it had witnessed something extraordinary.

She looked comfortable.

She looked beautiful.

And for a brief moment, seeing her in something that belonged to me made the entire evening feel more real.

We ordered food and sat together talking.

That was when she truly began opening up.

She told me stories about her life.

Stories about her experiences.

Stories about the reckless, funny, and sometimes completely ridiculous things she had done.

I listened to every word.

Not because I was trying to be polite.

But because I genuinely wanted to know.

I wanted to know who she was beyond the smile.

Beyond the pictures.

Beyond the first impression.

The more she talked, the more fascinated I became.

Hours passed, yet I never once felt bored.

In fact, I wished the conversation would continue forever.

Somewhere between the music, the laughter, the conversations, and the quiet moments, a thought settled into my mind.

A simple thought.

A dangerous thought.

She was different.

And for the first time, I found myself wanting someone not because of how they looked, but because of how they made me feel.

That night gave me more than a memory.

It gave me the first glimpse of her.

And somehow, that glimpse was enough to make me want to know everything else.
